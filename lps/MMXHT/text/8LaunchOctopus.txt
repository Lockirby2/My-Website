The stage itself was by far the easiest.  With the water physics, jumps are always fairly forgiving, and the mini-bosses are pretty easily defeated, although fun.  In hindsight, the way the ship falls probably worked similarly in vanilla in terms of revealing a secret, so I'm not sure what I was talking about at the one point.  There's not much else to say about that.  Actually, the easier stage means that I'm not distracted from what I'm saying, so my speech comes out as gibberish a little less often in this episode.

However, the boss managed to make up the difficulty here for me. I usually find it difficult when there are a lot of moving things onscreen (especially when they move fast), and this boss was no exception.  The Launch Octopus fight took me almost as much time to defeat as Sting Chameleon, but unlike Sting Chameleon, this fight is contending for being one of my favorites rather than my least favorite.  It's pretty intense and it feels like there's a lot of options available.  R. Shield comes in handy for me once I've sorted through the glib of using my other weapons, because it allows me to hit him when he's on the lower level and I'm on the higher one.  My other weapons ultimately ended up being a bigger distraction than a help.  Often I'd want to shoot my weapon, but then he'd fire his own weapons at me.  Then I'd feel the urge not to fire (in order to conserve ammo), and it would cause me to take a hit.  This fight seems to me like it would be the most dramatically changed if I had found the armor powerup on my first go through Sting Chameleon's stage.
